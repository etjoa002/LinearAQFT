# LinearAQFT

Algebraic quantum field theory of linear fields, formalized in Lean 4 over Mathlib: the net of
local C*-algebras of the free scalar field and the axioms of J. Dimock (Commun. Math. Phys. 77
(1980) 219–228), for an abstract classical phase space, for a lattice field on `ℤ²`, and for the
massless and Klein–Gordon fields on 3+1 Minkowski space.

The observables of a region `O` are generated by the Weyl operators of the test functions
localized in `O`. With the retarded and advanced Green operators `G^±` of the field equation
`P u = 0`, the commutator function `E = G⁻ − G⁺` (advanced minus retarded) defines a symplectic
form on the classes `[f]` of test functions, `σ([f], [g]) = ⟨f, E g⟩`, the Weyl operators obey
`W(u) W(w) = e^{−iσ(u,w)/2} W(u + w)`, and `𝒜(O)` is the norm closure of the star algebra
generated by the `W([f])` with `f` localized in `O`. The library proves that `O ↦ 𝒜(O)` is
generating, that algebras of causally disjoint regions commute, that the algebra of a region is
contained in that of any region on which it depends causally (time slice), and that symmetries
act covariantly; the existence of `G^±` enters as a hypothesis (Green data) and is proved for
the lattice and for Minkowski space.

## What is proved

| | Main results | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Causal indices and nets | Causal indices (orthogonality and dependence of regions) and their isomorphisms; nets of norm-closed star subalgebras; generation, causality, time slice and covariance as properties of a net; nets generated by sets of elements, causal when generators of orthogonal regions commute with each other and with each other's adjoints | Ch. 1 |
| The CCR algebra | The Weyl operators on `ℓ²(V)`, the Weyl relation; for alternating nondegenerate `σ` the CCR algebra is simple, and primitive given an irreducible representation (from BosonicLean) | Ch. 2, 6 |
| Phase-space data | For any phase-space data the CCR net, and the net of any Weyl system, is generating, causal and has the time slice; `𝒜(O)` is the closed span of its Weyl operators, and `W(v) ∈ 𝒜(O)` exactly when `v = [f]` with `f` localized in `O`; covariance under isomorphisms of phase-space data and under groups of them | Ch. 4, 6 |
| The lattice field | On `ℤ²`: the discrete Klein–Gordon operator, its Green operators and their uniqueness, the commutator function, the time slice through two consecutive slices; the axioms for every real `m²`, with simplicity unconditional; translation covariance; the net of Cauchy data, isomorphic to it | Ch. 3, 5, 6 |
| Green data | Green data (an operator with retarded and advanced Green operators satisfying `⟨f, G⁺g⟩ = ⟨g, G⁻f⟩`, and cutoffs) give a causal index and phase-space data with nondegenerate `σ`, hence the axioms, and covariance under their symmetries; the lattice is an instance | Ch. 7 |
| Minkowski space | The Kirchhoff operators are the Green operators of `∂ₜ² − Δ`, unique, with supports in `J^±`; the axioms (time slice for inertial slabs) and covariance under the orthochronous Poincaré group including space reflections | Ch. 8 |
| The Klein–Gordon field | For every real `μ` (`μ = m²` for mass `m`; negative values allowed) the series `G^±_μ = Σ_k (−μ)^k (G^±)^{k+1}` are the Green operators of `∂ₜ² − Δ + μ`; the axioms, uniqueness and covariance; Huygens' principle holds exactly when `μ = 0` | Ch. 9 |

The precise statements are the 64 theorems of [`verify/CORE.md`](verify/CORE.md), under headings
that refine the rows of this table. Primitivity is proved given an irreducible representation, whose existence
is not proved; the spacetime enters through Green data rather than a globally hyperbolic
manifold, and covariance is for time-orientation-preserving symmetries. Conventions are listed
in `CONVENTIONS.md`.

## Verification

The 64 theorems of the core are 28 main results, 31 statements that describe the library's
definitions in Mathlib's terms (some with facts such as formal self-adjointness, nondegeneracy
or the convergence of a series), and 5 examples (a nonzero value of the symplectic form on
the lattice, a pair of
regions in which one depends on the other without being contained in it, a field of nonzero
mass violating Huygens' principle, and others) in which the hypotheses hold and the objects are
not degenerate. `verify/Challenge.lean` states them without proofs, and `verify/Solution.lean`
proves them from the library. The command

```
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
```

checks that every proved theorem has exactly the stated form, that no axioms beyond `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` are used, and that Lean's kernel and four independently
written proof checkers accept every proof. A reader who accepts the 64 statements therefore need
not read any proof. Besides the definitions described in the core, two kinds of structure are
taken from the libraries: the C*-algebra structure of the CCR algebra, a closed star subalgebra
of the bounded operators on `ℓ²(V)`, and the group structures of the automorphisms of a causal
index and of the orthochronous Poincaré maps, whose products the core describes. The same check
runs on GitHub at every change.

`lake comparator` is the proof judge maintained by the Lean developers for proofs whose author is
not trusted, including proofs produced by AI; see
[leanprover/comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) and the chapter
[Validating a Lean Proof](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/#validating-comparator)
of the Lean reference manual. Among its checkers are
[lean4lean](https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean) and [nanoda](https://github.com/ammkrn/nanoda_lib).
On Linux it isolates the compiled code in a sandbox; elsewhere it needs the option
`--inadvisably-no-sandbox`.

## Build and verify

With the Lean installer `elan`:

```
git clone https://github.com/etjoa002/LinearAQFT
cd LinearAQFT
lake exe cache get          # download the precompiled Mathlib
lake build LinearAQFT
lake comparator --config verify/comparator.json --paranoid
lake build LinearAQFTDimock # optional: the three slices, with their axioms printed
```

The library depends on [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4),
[LocalAlgebrasLean](https://github.com/etjoa002/LocalAlgebrasLean) (regions, nets of local
algebras) and [BosonicLean](https://github.com/etjoa002/BosonicLean) (the CCR algebra, Weyl
systems, simplicity); their versions, and that of Lean, are fixed in `lakefile.toml`,
`lake-manifest.json` and `lean-toolchain`, and are only ever changed together.

## Methods of proof

For phase-space data the axioms follow by short algebra from properties of the generators: Weyl
operators of causally disjoint test functions commute because `σ` vanishes on them, and the time
slice of `σ` puts each generator of a dependent region into the later algebra. Green data yield
phase-space data, the time slice coming from the identity `f − f' = P((1−χ) G⁺ f + χ G⁻ f)` for
a cutoff `χ`. The analysis is confined to constructing Green data: by recursion on the lattice,
by Kirchhoff's formula and spherical means for the wave operator, and by a convergent series in
the massless Green operators for the Klein–Gordon operator. Simplicity of the CCR algebra comes
from averaging conjugations by Weyl operators, which converge to `τ(a)·1`.

## Numerical checks

The scripts in `checks/` test the formulas and sign conventions of the library numerically,
exactly where the arithmetic allows (integers, rationals, SymPy) and to stated tolerances
otherwise: `lattice_check.py` and `lattice_net_check.py` the lattice propagators, the
commutator function and the Cauchy-data net, `weyl_check.py` and `simplicity_check.py` the Weyl
operators and the averaging argument, `green_data_check.py` the Green data of the lattice,
`kirchhoff_check.py` the Kirchhoff operators and `klein_gordon_check.py` the Klein–Gordon series
and Huygens' principle. Each runs as `python -X utf8 checks/<name>.py` (Python 3 with NumPy;
SymPy and SciPy for two of them; `checks/requirements.txt`) and prints PASS or FAIL for every
check; all take seconds except
`kirchhoff_check.py`, which takes a few minutes. The example of the notes marked "script ✓" is
verified by `lattice_net_check.py`.

## Library layout

`Causal/` and `Net/` causal indices and nets; `Symplectic/` phase-space data and the CCR net;
`Lattice/` the lattice field; `Green/` Green data; `Minkowski/` the fields on 3+1 Minkowski
space; `Analysis/` lemmas of real analysis; `Deprecated.lean` earlier names; outside the library,
`verify/` the core, `slices/` the three slices for the axioms of Dimock (1980) (abstract,
lattice, Minkowski space), each with the exact set of library files it
needs (`slices/README.md`), and `checks/` the numerical checks.

## Status

The library is under development, and this repository is updated as the work progresses; see
`CONTRIBUTING.md` for problem reports and suggestions. Planned next is flat-space algebraic
quantum field theory at the level of current lecture notes: states and their GNS
representations, the polynomial field algebra, and the time slice in Cauchy-surface form.
Candidates for later theorems: an irreducible representation of the CCR algebra, which would
remove the hypothesis of primitivity; the uniqueness of the CCR algebra up to isomorphism
(J. Slawny, Commun. Math. Phys. 24 (1972) 151–170) and hence independence of the
representation; the time slice for curved Cauchy surfaces in Minkowski space; Green operators
on globally hyperbolic spacetimes.
